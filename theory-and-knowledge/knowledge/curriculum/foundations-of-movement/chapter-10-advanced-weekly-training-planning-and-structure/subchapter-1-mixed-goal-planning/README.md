# Mixed-Goal Planning

## Overview
Some users do not have a single goal. They want strength, movement quality, and consistency at the same time. Mixed-goal planning requires choosing priorities without losing the plan's clarity.

## Key Ideas
- more than one goal can be valid
- goals need a priority order when time is limited
- mixed plans work best when each session has a role
- clarity is more useful than trying to maximize everything at once

## Connection to Practice
This lesson helps the learner explain how a plan can support multiple outcomes without becoming chaotic.

## Knowledge Check
Why does a mixed-goal plan still need a primary priority?

## Scenario
A learner works through a case involving subchapter 1 mixed goal planning. They should explain what changes, why it matters, and what the next training decision should be.
