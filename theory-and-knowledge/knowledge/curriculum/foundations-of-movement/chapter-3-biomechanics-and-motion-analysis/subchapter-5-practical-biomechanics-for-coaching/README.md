# Practical Biomechanics for Coaching

## Overview
This subchapter connects the earlier concepts back to coaching decisions. It helps the learner ask better questions: What is the movement pattern? How much load is being created? Where is the body losing control or efficiency? What should be changed next?

## Key Ideas
- coaching is not just about preparing for effort; it is about making movement understandable
- exercise cues should match the movement goal, not just the athlete's motivation
- a reasonable movement analysis is built from basic concepts, not exhaustive mechanics models
- the best training plan is the one the learner can explain and repeat

## Connection to Practice
A trainee who knows how to describe a movement pattern and identify force demand is much more likely to follow a well-structured program without chasing random intensity.
