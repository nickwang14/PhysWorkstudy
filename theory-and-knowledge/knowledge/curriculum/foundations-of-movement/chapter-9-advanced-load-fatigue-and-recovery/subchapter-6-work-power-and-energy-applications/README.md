# Work, Power, and Energy Applications

## Overview
This lesson applies the work, power, and energy concepts to training decisions. The aim is to make these ideas useful for programming and coaching rather than just theoretical.

## Key Ideas
- work can help describe the demand of repeated movement
- power explains how quickly effort is expressed
- energy cost can differ across movement style and speed
- practical programming needs the concept, not the math alone

## Connection to Practice
The learner should be able to explain why a movement can be mechanically similar but feel very different in output or effort.

## Scenario
Two users perform the same exercise, but one moves slowly with high control and the other moves quickly with less control. The learner should explain what changes in work, power, and energy demand.
