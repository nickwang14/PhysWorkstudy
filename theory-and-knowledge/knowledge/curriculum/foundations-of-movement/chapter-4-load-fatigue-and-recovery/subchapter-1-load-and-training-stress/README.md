# Load and Training Stress

## Overview
Training creates stress. The goal is not to avoid stress entirely, but to manage it so the body can adapt without becoming overloaded or chronically depleted.

## Key Ideas
- load is the amount of work or effort applied in a training session or block
- stress is not automatically harmful; it becomes a problem when recovery and consistency are not managed
- effective training balances stimulus and recovery rather than maxing effort every time
- the same exercise can create different stress depending on volume, intensity, and recovery status

## Connection to Practice
This subchapter helps the learner understand why a week with two hard sessions and little recovery can be worse than a moderate week that repeats consistently.

## Knowledge Check
How can the same exercise feel easy in one week and overwhelming in another week?

## Scenario
A learner works through a case involving subchapter 1 load and training stress. They should explain what changes, why it matters, and what the next training decision should be.
