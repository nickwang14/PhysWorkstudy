# Progression Rules

## Overview
Progression is not a random increase in effort. It is a deliberate plan to increase challenge in a way that remains consistent with the user's goal, recovery status, and schedule.

## Key Ideas
- progression should be gradual and explainable
- volume, intensity, or complexity can change, but they should not all change at once without a reason
- improvement is often a result of repeated quality, not larger single-session effort
- poor progression is usually careless change, not a sign of ambition

## Connection to Practice
This subchapter helps the learner understand why a stable program often works better than constant experimentation.

## Knowledge Check
If a user feels good and complete, what is a safer progression decision: increase everything immediately or make one carefully chosen change?

## Scenario
A learner works through a case involving subchapter 2 progression rules. They should explain what changes, why it matters, and what the next training decision should be.
