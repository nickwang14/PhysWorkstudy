# Tissue Types and Movement Systems

## Overview
Movement depends on more than muscles. The body uses several tissue types to generate force, transmit load, and maintain stability. A useful foundation understands how these tissues connect to one another.

## Key Ideas
- connective tissue supports and links structures
- muscle tissue produces tension and movement
- bone tissue provides structure and lever support
- fascia and soft tissues help distribute load
- the movement system works as a coordinated chain, not isolated parts

## Connection to Practice
When a learner understands tissue types, it becomes easier to see why stress, posture, and recovery all matter. Movement quality is not just about effort; it is about how the body manages force through structure.

## Scenario
A learner works through a case involving subchapter 1 tissue types and movement systems. They should explain what changes, why it matters, and what the next training decision should be.
