# Tissue Organization and Load Sharing

The musculoskeletal system works because tissues are organized into connected chains rather than isolated structures. Bone provides the rigid framework, connective tissue binds and transfers force, muscle generates tension, and cartilage and fascia help smooth and distribute stress. This is why a movement is never just one tissue doing one job.

A useful way to think about the system is that the body tries to distribute load across the most efficient and stable pathways available. When tissues are organized well, force is shared more evenly. When tissues are overloaded or poorly organized, stress may become concentrated in one area, increasing the risk of poor movement or discomfort.

## Why It Matters
This concept helps explain why movement quality depends on coordination and alignment. Training is not just about making tissues stronger in isolation; it is also about helping the entire body manage load through an integrated system.
