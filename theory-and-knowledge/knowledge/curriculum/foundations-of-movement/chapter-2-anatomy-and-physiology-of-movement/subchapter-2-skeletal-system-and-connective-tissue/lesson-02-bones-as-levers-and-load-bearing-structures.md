# Bones as Levers and Load-Bearing Structures

Bones are not only rigid support elements. They also act as levers that allow the body to move around joints. The skeleton gives the body shape, but it also makes movement possible by creating a framework against which muscles can pull and generate motion.

This lever system matters because force and movement are not created by muscles alone. The skeleton helps position segments, provides attachment sites, and changes the mechanical advantage of the movement. When a lever is longer or the force is applied at a different angle, the movement demands and mechanical efficiency change.

## Why It Matters
A learner who understands bones as levers can reason more clearly about why some joint positions feel harder than others and why technique matters. The same muscle can produce different demands depending on how the bones and joints are positioned.

## Key Terms
- lever
- lever arm
- mechanical advantage
- load-bearing structure
- bone position
- force application
