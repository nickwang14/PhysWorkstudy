# Agonists, Antagonists, and Synergists

Muscles rarely work in isolation. During a simple movement, one group may act as the primary mover while other muscles help control the movement or support the joint. This is why we often describe muscles by their role in a movement.

An agonist is the main muscle or muscle group producing the movement. An antagonist opposes the action and helps control the range and speed of movement. A synergist assists the agonist, either by helping produce force or by stabilizing nearby joints. This coordination allows the body to move with more control.

## Why It Matters
Understanding muscle roles helps explain how a body moves with precision, not just brute force. It also helps explain why training often emphasizes control, coordination, and balance rather than isolated effort alone.

## Key Terms
- agonist
- antagonist
- synergist
- primary mover
- coordination
- joint control
