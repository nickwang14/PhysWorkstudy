# Joint Stability and Articulation

A joint is where two or more bones meet, and articulation describes that meeting point. The body depends on joints to create movement while still maintaining some degree of control and protection. This is why joint structure is not just about motion range — it is also about stability.

Stability comes from the shape of the joint, the surrounding connective tissues, and the muscles that help balance the joint under load. Some joints are designed for large ranges of motion, while others emphasize stability and control. A shoulder may allow more range, while the knee may prioritize controlled force acceptance and alignment.

## Why It Matters
Understanding joint articulation helps explain why exercise performance depends on both mobility and stability. Better movement often comes from matching the right movement to the right joint capacity and control demands.
