# Joints and Leverage

## Overview
Joints make movement possible, and leverage describes how forces act around a joint to create motion. The musculoskeletal system works like a set of levers connected by joints, with muscles generating force and bones acting as the rigid lever arms.

## Key Ideas
- joints are the pivot points for movement
- lever systems influence how much force is required for a given task
- muscle line of pull and joint position affect movement efficiency
- load and posture shape the demand placed on tissues

## Connection to Practice
This subchapter links anatomy with biomechanics by explaining how joint structure and segment position shape movement demands. Posture alone does not establish tissue damage or predict injury. Content is educational, not diagnostic.

## Scenario
A learner compares carrying a grocery bag close to the body with holding it forward. The bag's weight is unchanged, but its line of action changes the shoulder's turning demand. Bringing it closer can make the task more manageable without diagnosing the shoulder.
