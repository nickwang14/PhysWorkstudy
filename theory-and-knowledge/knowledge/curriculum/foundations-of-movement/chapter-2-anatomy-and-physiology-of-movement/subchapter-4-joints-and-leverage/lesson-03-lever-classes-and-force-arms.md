# Lever Classes and Force Arms

Human bodies use simple lever principles to move segments around joints. A lever consists of a rigid segment, a pivot, and forces acting at different distances from that pivot. In the body, joints act as pivots, bones act as rigid segments, and muscles create force at specific points.

There are three basic lever classes. In a first-class lever, the pivot sits between the force and the resistance. In a second-class lever, the resistance is between the pivot and the force. In a third-class lever, the force is between the pivot and the resistance. Most human movement is dominated by third-class arrangements, where muscles act close to the joint while the load is farther away. This often means the body must generate significant force to move a limb or resist a load.

## Why It Matters
Lever mechanics explain why certain joint positions feel easier or harder and why a small force at one point can create a larger effect on a distant segment. This concept helps convert anatomy into practical reasoning about task difficulty and exercise technique.
