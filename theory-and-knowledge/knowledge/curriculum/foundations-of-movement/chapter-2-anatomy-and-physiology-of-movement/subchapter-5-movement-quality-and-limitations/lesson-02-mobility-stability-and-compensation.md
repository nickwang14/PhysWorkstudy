# Mobility, Stability, and Compensation

Mobility is the ability to move through a range of motion. Stability is the ability to control that motion and hold position under load. These ideas are related but not identical. A person may have good mobility but poor stability, or good stability but limited mobility. Both matter.

Compensation happens when the body changes the way it moves to make up for a lack of mobility, stability, or control. For example, if the hips cannot move well, the lower back may compensate. If the trunk cannot stabilize well, the shoulders or knees may compensate. This is why movement quality is not just about whether a movement is completed; it is about how the body achieves it.

## Why It Matters
Movement quality depends on the relationship between mobility and stability. A program should not just push for more range or more effort. It should help the person move in a way that is safe, coordinated, and sustainable.
