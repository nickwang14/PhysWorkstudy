# Movement Quality and Limitations

## Overview
Movement quality describes how well the body controls a task under its current load, pace, and fatigue. It includes coordination, task-appropriate alignment, repeatability, and manageable effort. A changed strategy is not automatically harmful compensation, and appearance alone cannot diagnose injury.

## Key Ideas
- movement quality is more than range of motion or effort
- mobility and stability both matter
- tissue tolerance and fatigue shape what a person can do well
- exercise should match the user's current movement capabilities and task goals

## Connection to Practice
This subchapter bridges anatomy to practical training decisions. A useful plan does not just push harder; it respects current capability, control, and recovery. Content is educational, not diagnostic.

## Scenario
A learner's step-up descent becomes rushed late in a set. A pause and a lower step improve repeatability. That supports choosing a more manageable task, but does not establish whether fatigue, attention, balance demand, or another factor caused the change.
