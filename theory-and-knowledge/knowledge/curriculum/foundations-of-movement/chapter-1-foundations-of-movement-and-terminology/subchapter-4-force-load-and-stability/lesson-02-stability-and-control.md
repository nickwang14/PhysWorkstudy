# Stability and Control

Stability is the ability to maintain posture and control motion under the stress of force. In training, stability matters because a movement is not just “successful” because it is heavy or hard. It is successful when the body can create and control force in a coordinated way.

Good stability is not the absence of motion. It is the ability to direct motion and resist unnecessary movement. For example, a strong squat is not only about lowering weight; it is about maintaining control through the trunk, hips, knees, and ankles while the body moves through a range of motion.

## Why It Matters
When users do not understand stability, they often focus on intensity, novelty, or speed instead of control. But a stable movement pattern allows the user to learn better, adapt better, and recover more consistently. That is the connection between all the earlier terms: force, load, movement, stability, and exercise selection.
