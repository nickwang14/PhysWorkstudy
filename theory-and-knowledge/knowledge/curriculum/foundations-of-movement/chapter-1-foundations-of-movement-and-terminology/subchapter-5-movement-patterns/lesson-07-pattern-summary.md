# Pattern Summary

Movement patterns give the learner a way to categorize exercises based on the movement demand rather than random exercise names. The major patterns include squat, hinge, push, pull, carry, and rotation/anti-rotation.

A useful program does not just stack random exercises. It uses a pattern-based lens to ask what movement demands are being trained, what is being balanced, and what recovery and control needs exist. This helps the learner move from “I am doing a workout” to “I understand the structure and purpose behind the workout.”

## Key Takeaways
- patterns are a framework, not just a list
- movement quality depends on control, stability, and force management
- exercise selection is more meaningful when explained by pattern and purpose
- a good program balances multiple patterns rather than chasing novelty alone
