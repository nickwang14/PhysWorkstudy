# Pattern Overview

Movement patterns are repeatable ways that the body organizes force and control. Instead of thinking about exercises as isolated tasks, it can be more useful to think of them as patterns: squat, hinge, push, pull, carry, and rotate/anti-rotate.

This framework helps learners make sense of a training plan without memorizing random exercise names. Once the user understands movement patterns, they can more easily reason about exercise selection, muscle involvement, and program quality.

## Why It Matters
Patterns help explain the logic behind training. For example, a squat pattern and a deadlift pattern may both challenge lower body strength, but they involve different movement demands and different control demands. This is why exercise selection should not be based only on what looks impressive or what is trendy.

## Key Patterns
- squat pattern
- hinge pattern
- push pattern
- pull pattern
- carry pattern
- rotation / anti-rotation pattern
