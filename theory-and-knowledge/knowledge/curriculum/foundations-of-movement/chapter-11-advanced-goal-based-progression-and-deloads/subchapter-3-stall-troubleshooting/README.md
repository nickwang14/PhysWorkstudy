# Stall Troubleshooting

## Overview
When progress stalls, the next step is not always to push harder. Troubleshooting looks at what part of the plan is no longer fitting the user's recovery, consistency, or goal.

## Key Ideas
- stalls can come from load, recovery, schedule, or planning mismatch
- one stalled week is not the same as a true plateau
- troubleshooting should be systematic
- the goal is to restore momentum without creating noise

## Connection to Practice
This lesson helps the learner decide whether to adjust exercise choice, load, frequency, or rest.

## Knowledge Check
What is the first thing to ask when a plan stalls: "How do we force more?" or "What is no longer fitting?"

## Scenario
A learner works through a case involving subchapter 3 stall troubleshooting. They should explain what changes, why it matters, and what the next training decision should be.
