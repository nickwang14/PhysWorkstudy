# Preserving Momentum Through Change

## Overview
Good program changes should keep the user moving forward. This lesson focuses on how to change the plan without breaking the habit or the sense of progress.

## Key Ideas
- momentum matters in long-term adherence
- changes should be understandable and minimal when possible
- preserving routine reduces friction
- the best change is often the smallest one that solves the problem

## Connection to Practice
This lesson connects progression logic to behavior and consistency.

## Knowledge Check
Why is a small, explainable change often better than a large reset?

## Scenario
A learner works through a case involving subchapter 5 preserving momentum through change. They should explain what changes, why it matters, and what the next training decision should be.
