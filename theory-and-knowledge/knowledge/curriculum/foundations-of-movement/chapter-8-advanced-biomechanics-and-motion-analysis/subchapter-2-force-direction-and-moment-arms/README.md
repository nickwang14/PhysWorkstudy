# Force Direction and Moment Arms

## Overview
This subchapter adds a deeper look at how force direction and leverage change the mechanical demand of a movement. Small changes in position can create large changes in effort.

## Key Ideas
- force direction affects whether a movement is easier or harder to control
- moment arms change the leverage demand on joints and muscles
- positioning can alter the same exercise without changing the exercise name
- efficiency depends on how force is applied relative to the task

## Connection to Practice
The learner should be able to explain why a seemingly minor stance, grip, or torso-angle change can meaningfully alter load.

## Knowledge Check
Why does a longer moment arm usually make a movement more demanding even if the external load stays the same?

## Scenario
A learner works through a case involving subchapter 2 force direction and moment arms. They should explain what changes, why it matters, and what the next training decision should be.
