# Efficiency, Control, and Trade-Offs

## Overview
At an advanced level, the learner should understand that better control is not always the same as maximum efficiency. Training often requires trade-offs between stability, power, range, and repeatability.

## Key Ideas
- efficiency means accomplishing the task with sensible mechanical cost
- control means the movement remains stable and predictable
- trade-offs are common when a user wants both safety and performance
- the best choice depends on the goal and the stage of learning

## Connection to Practice
This subchapter helps the learner understand why a coach may choose a simpler movement pattern to protect quality or consistency.
