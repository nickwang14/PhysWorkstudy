# Movement Variability and Constraints

## Overview
Advanced biomechanics starts by recognizing that movement is not perfectly fixed. Variability is normal, and constraints from the body, task, and environment shape how a movement looks and feels.

## Key Ideas
- movement changes across reps, sessions, and contexts
- constraints can come from mobility, fatigue, load, equipment, or time
- the goal is not identical repetition but repeatable control
- variability can be useful when it stays inside the movement goal

## Connection to Practice
This lesson helps the learner explain why two reps can both be valid even if they do not look identical.

## Knowledge Check
Which matters more in an advanced coaching context: identical form on every rep, or stable control inside the movement goal? Why?

## Scenario
A learner works through a case involving subchapter 1 movement variability and constraints. They should explain what changes, why it matters, and what the next training decision should be.
