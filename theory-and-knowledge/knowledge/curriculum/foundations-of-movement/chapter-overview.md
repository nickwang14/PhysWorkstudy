# Foundations of Movement — Chapter Overview

## Program Theme
This program is the foundation layer for the PhysiApp learning path. It teaches the concepts and terminology needed to understand how the body moves, how training creates stress, and how to make reasonable, evidence-informed decisions about workouts and recovery.

## Core Learning Sequence
The app should present this as a continuous learning ladder rather than a school calendar. Each chapter unlocks the next chapter once the learner completes the required concept gate.

### 1. Foundations of Movement and Terminology
**Purpose:** Build the vocabulary and mental model for how the body moves.

**Major concepts:**
- movement patterns
- anatomy basics
- planes and axes
- joint movement terminology
- force, load, and muscle action

**Primary references:**
- Anatomy and Physiology 2e
- Biomechanics of Human Movement
- Foundations of Exercise Science

### 2. Anatomy and Physiology of Movement
**Purpose:** Understand the structure and function of the musculoskeletal system.

**Major concepts:**
- tissue types and movement systems
- joints and leverage
- muscular action and contraction types
- connective tissue and support systems
- movement quality and limitations

**Primary references:**
- Anatomy and Physiology 2e
- Body Physics

### 3. Biomechanics and Motion Analysis
**Purpose:** Learn how motion and forces determine exercise quality and performance.

**Major concepts:**
- linear and angular motion
- kinematics and kinetics
- force vectors and momentum
- work, power, and energy
- practical movement analysis

**Primary references:**
- Biomechanics of Human Movement
- Body Physics

### 4. Load, Fatigue, and Recovery
**Purpose:** Teach the user how training stress interacts with adaptation and recovery.

**Major concepts:**
- load and intensity
- fatigue and adaptation
- recovery windows
- deload logic
- the difference between soreness, stress, and injury risk

**Primary references:**
- Body Physics
- Foundations of Exercise Science
- Biomechanics of Human Movement

### 5. Weekly Training Planning and Structure
**Purpose:** Turn the science into a practical weekly plan.

**Major concepts:**
- session frequency and scheduling flexibility
- exercise selection by goal
- split design and user availability
- weekly structure and rest-day logic
- minimum effective consistency

**Primary references:**
- Foundations of Exercise Science
- Body Physics
- Program design standards from exercise science frameworks

### 6. Goal-Based Progression and Deloads
**Purpose:** Teach how to adapt the program without chasing intensity.

**Major concepts:**
- progression rules
- weekly goal tracking
- deloads and planned recovery
- busyness and schedule adaptation
- reasoned progression versus random changes

**Primary references:**
- Foundations of Exercise Science
- Exercise science periodization references
- PhysiApp-specific deload policy

### 7. Applied Exercise Decision-Making and Coaching Logic
**Purpose:** Convert knowledge into decision-making for real-world training.

**Major concepts:**
- when to change exercise selection
- when to reduce volume or intensity
- how to explain a program decision
- coaching logic for flexibility, consistency, and recovery
- safety and non-medical boundary framing

**Primary references:**
- Exercise science frameworks
- Physio consultant program standards
- Applied coaching texts and educational material

## Gate Logic
Each chapter should unlock the next only after the learner demonstrates enough understanding through:
- short quiz or concept check
- one practical application prompt
- completion of a short learning commitment (e.g., daily lesson + summary)

This creates a progression map that behaves like a learning game without being superficial.

## Advanced Curriculum Sequence
After the foundation path, the curriculum can continue into a second set of chapters that reuse the same subject areas but with more depth, more cases, and more technical reasoning. These chapters are part of the active curriculum plan, not a review overlay.

### 8. Advanced Biomechanics and Motion Analysis
**Purpose:** expand the motion-analysis toolkit with more nuanced reasoning and applied biomechanics.

**Major concepts:**
- movement variability and constraint-based analysis
- force direction, moment arms, and efficiency trade-offs
- simple force-time reasoning
- movement interpretation across multiple rep types and tasks
- practical case studies for coaching and form review

### 9. Advanced Load, Fatigue, and Recovery
**Purpose:** deepen the understanding of training stress, adaptation, and recovery management.

**Major concepts:**
- volume, intensity, frequency, and fatigue interaction
- recovery markers and cumulative stress
- adaptation signals across weeks and blocks
- fatigue management across changing life demands
- when recovery data should change the plan

### 10. Advanced Weekly Training Planning and Structure
**Purpose:** build more complex weekly systems for variable schedules, mixed goals, and user-specific constraints.

**Major concepts:**
- multi-goal weekly planning
- split architecture trade-offs
- travel weeks, low-time weeks, and re-entry weeks
- session sequencing under constraint
- maintaining consistency while changing structure

### 11. Advanced Goal-Based Progression and Deloads
**Purpose:** teach progression as a decision system rather than a single rule set.

**Major concepts:**
- autoregulation and progression selection
- plan stalling and adjustment logic
- deload timing by signal, not habit alone
- progression model comparison
- how to preserve momentum while changing load

### 12. Advanced Applied Exercise Decision-Making and Coaching Logic
**Purpose:** synthesize the earlier curriculum into advanced case-based coaching reasoning.

**Major concepts:**
- decision trees for program changes
- regression and progression in complex scenarios
- coaching language for explanation and trust
- case review using real-world constraint patterns
- advanced consistency-first programming logic

These advanced chapters reuse the same educational language as the core path, but they assume the learner already completed the foundation sequence and is ready for deeper review, more scenario work, and more technical decision-making.
